# Field guide: grants for the non-profit

Researched October 2026. Deadlines, rules and policies change: check each programme's own page before acting.

How to fill any grant application well, built from the pages listed in section 7. Facts about the applying
organisation come only from the applicant's profile file `desk/profile.md`. Where two sources disagree, both are
given and the conflict is named.

Three words used throughout. **Gate**: a yes/no rule that ends an application before anyone scores it.
**Grid**: the scoring sheet a reviewer fills in. **Ask**: the amount requested.

---

## 1. How grant reviewers decide

**1. Gates come first, and most applications die there.** Every grid opened for this guide starts with a checklist,
not a judgment. Enabel's EU-style grid has a yes/no administrative check (right form, signed, annexes attached,
budget balanced and in the stated currency, logframe attached) before a single point is given. The UK DWP grid
rejects outright on any "No" in the selection questions. Bolton CVS lists ineligibility and missed criteria as the
top two reasons for decline. Fit and completeness end more applications than quality does.

**2. Scores are sums of small, separate questions.** Reviewers do not grade an essay. They score sub-questions,
usually on a 1–5 or 0–6 scale, then add them. Enabel scores 1 (very inadequate) to 5 (very good) per line; a 1, 2 or
5 must be justified in writing. DWP uses 0, 2, 4, 6. MacArthur's 100&Change used 0.1–5.0 bands per criterion. So
each sub-question needs its own visible answer. A strong paragraph that skips one sub-question still loses that line.

**3. Some lines carry double weight, and some have their own floor.** Enabel doubles relevance to the call and to
local needs at concept-note stage, and doubles "activities reflected in the budget" at full stage. It also sets
floors inside the total: 40 of 70 overall, but also 6 of 10 on relevance to the call and 3 of 5 on target-group
definition. At full stage, below 12 of 20 on financial and operational capacity is elimination, whatever the rest
scores. DWP fails any question scoring 0 and needs at least 2 on each. Read the floors before you read the weights.

**4. The weight pattern repeats across very different funders.**

| Grid | Capacity | Relevance / need | Design / feasibility | Sustainability | Budget / value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Enabel full proposal (EU PRAG style), 100 points, pass 60 | 20 (floor 12) | 30 (carried from concept note) | 20 | 15 | 15 (budget line doubled) |
| Enabel concept note, 70 points, pass 40 | — | 35 (two lines doubled; ESG plan 5 points) | 35 (scale-up line doubled) | inside design | — |
| MacArthur 100&Change, four equal criteria | Feasible | Impactful | Evidence-based | Durable | inside Feasible |
| Commonwealth Foundation open call, eight criteria | track record; capacity to deliver | clear problem; beneficiaries consulted | clear approach; persuasive, timely, feasible | — | — (gender equality is its own criterion) |
| US Embassy PDS, 100 points | 10 | 20 | 20 + M&E 20 | 10 | 20 |

Relevance and design take about half the points almost everywhere. Capacity is small in points but often a floor.

**5. Sustainability is the line everyone writes worst.** MacArthur reported that "durable" had the lowest median
score of its four criteria; many applicants skipped it or answered vaguely. Its rubric rewards a defined path:
a problem solved in five years, or a route that attracts other private, public or philanthropic money. Enabel splits
sustainability into financial, institutional (local ownership), policy and environmental parts. Answer each named
part, even in one line.

**6. Evidence that it worked once, somewhere, at some scale.** MacArthur's evidence criterion asks whether the
solution or its parts have already produced concrete results, and whether they will hold in a new context.
Plinth names thin impact evidence as a leading decline reason across UK funders. Proof of a past result beats a
promise of a future one.

**7. Proximity is now scored openly.** Skoll lists proximity to the problem as a criterion. Echoing Green scores
"relationship to and expertise on the issue": reflecting the community, designing with it, shifting power to it.
Global Fund for Children and Global Fund for Women look for groups led by people with lived experience. Comic
Relief asks how people with lived experience take part. The Pollination Project's advisors weigh personal connection
to the problem. An applicant's roots in the community served are a scored asset, not colour. State them as evidence.

**8. Community voice must be shown, not claimed.** The National Lottery told Connected Voice that applications too
often skip how the community was consulted. Its advice: name the community, say how many people you asked, how,
and what they said. Commonwealth scores "evidence of consulting intended beneficiaries" as its own criterion.
Big Lottery declines cited "lack of involvement from project beneficiaries" (Bolton CVS factsheet).

**9. Most big money is not open.** Ford says fewer than 1% of unsolicited applications are funded in a typical
year; its officers mostly find grantees. OSF says most grants go to organisations it approaches. Skoll selects by
nomination over several years. Wellspring takes no unsolicited requests and closes in 2027. Segal sources through
referral networks, partners, peer funders and embassy small-grant contacts. Mastercard Foundation works through
partners and partner-run calls. For these, the "application" is a relationship and a findable record.

**10. The trust-based minority asks less and reads differently.** Trust-based funders do their own research and
cut paperwork. Global Fund for Children and Global Fund for Women take a profile, then invite. Kwanda puts each
proposal to a member vote, with cost, builder and expected outcome shown. Awesome chapters decide monthly, sometimes
at public pitch nights. Here clarity and community benefit beat rubric language.

**11. Reviewers are often not specialists and read quickly.** A foundation insider writing in Alliance warns that
proposals may reach board members outside the field, so jargon and acronyms cost points. Comic Relief caps its
summary at 350 characters and asks for no jargon and no context in it. The Plinth guide reports Esmée Fairbairn
invited only 7% of expressions of interest to full proposal in 2024. A first screen is short and harsh.

**12. Small-grant panels judge the person and the plan together.** Pollination advisors weigh personal connection,
ripple effects, community support, a realistic budget with non-cash resources, and a clear path for the money.
Peace First wants action already under way. Awesome wants community benefit and an idea a small sum moves forward.
None of them want a polished institution. They want a credible person with a plan the money visibly advances.

## 2. The questions you will be asked

Grouped by what they test. For each: what the reviewer checks, then the answer structure. Structures, not prose.
Build every answer from the record; mark gaps `[TBC]`.

### A. Summary and fit

**"Summarise your proposal" (Comic Relief: 2–3 sentences, 350 characters; Azrieli: 600 characters).**
Tests: can you say what changes, for whom, in plain words. Structure: who benefits → what will change for them →
what you will do → where and how long. No context, no history, no activity list.

**"How does this fit our priorities / the call?"**
Tests: relevance, often the heaviest line (Enabel doubles it). Structure: name the priority in the funder's own
words → the one mechanism by which your work advances it → one piece of evidence. Pick one priority and name it
(National Lottery advice). Do not list every SDG.

**"Has this work been funded before? By whom?"** (Comic Relief asks both.)
Tests: track record and duplication. Structure: yes/no → funder, year, amount → what it produced → report
available. Be ready to upload the last report or evaluation.

### B. Need and people

**"Describe the people who will benefit and the problems they face" (Comic Relief: 3,500 characters).**
Tests: specificity and evidence. Structure: who (age, place, number) → the problem in their lives → the forces
behind it (people, systems, policies) → recent evidence (national, then local, then your own) → one consented
voice. Comic Relief asks for the major factors shaping their lives, not only symptoms.

**"How were beneficiaries involved / consulted?"**
Tests: participation (Commonwealth, Comic Relief, Lottery, Echoing Green). Structure: how many people → method
(survey, meeting, class feedback) → when → what they said → what you changed because of it → how they take part in
decisions during the project.

**"Why now?"** (Skoll's "inflection point"; Echoing Green asks why this moment.)
Tests: urgency. Structure: what has shifted (policy, technology, demand) → what is lost if you wait → why your
timing is right.

### C. Change and design

**"What changes do you expect?" (Comic Relief: 3,500 characters).**
Tests: outcomes, not outputs. Structure: change 1, 2, 3 (capacity, behaviour, structures, services or policy) →
for whom → by when → how you will know. The Lottery's top complaint is activities written as impact.

**"What will you do?" (activities, 3,500 characters) and "Why will this work?" (2,100 characters).**
Tests: logic and evidence. Structure for the first: activities in sequence, with who, where, how often. For the
second: the assumption behind each step → evidence it holds (your past result, others' research) → what you will do
if it does not.

**"Theory of change / logic model"** (Commonwealth requires its own logic model template.)
Tests: the "missing middle" between activity and goal. Structure: long-term goal → preconditions that must be true,
mapped backwards → interventions that cause them → indicators for progress on goals, not only outputs → assumptions
named. (Center for Theory of Change method.)

**"What makes your approach different?"** (Echoing Green, Google.org, Ashoka-type panels.)
Tests: awareness of the field. Structure: what exists → where it falls short for this group → what you do
differently → who else works on it and how you would work with them. Echoing Green scores awareness of others'
work and asks for a "collaborative analysis" of similar organisations.

**"How will you scale or replicate?"** (Enabel doubles the scale-up line; Google.org scores scalability.)
Tests: a path beyond the pilot. Structure: the unit (school, cohort, community) → its cost → the next three units
→ who could run it without you → the policy or public system it could plug into (Enabel scores alignment with public
strategies and local buy-in).

### D. Measurement

**"How will you measure success?"**
Tests: objectively verifiable indicators and a planned evaluation (Enabel line 20). Structure: indicator →
baseline → target → source → who collects → how often. Add an end evaluation, even a simple one. Smaller groups
lose because they cannot show evidence of past work (Connected Voice).

**"What will you learn / how will results inform practice?"** (Comic Relief asks how frontline delivery informs
policy.) Structure: the question you are testing → how results reach practitioners or policymakers.

### E. Organisation and capacity

**"What is your purpose and what are you most proud of? Why are you well placed?" (Comic Relief: 2,100 characters).**
Tests: credibility. Structure: purpose in one line → two or three dated achievements with numbers →
experience with this group and this problem → management capacity (staff, systems, prior grants handled).

**"Experience managing projects / technical expertise / management capacity / stable funding"** (Enabel's four
capacity lines, 5 points each, floor 12 of 20.) Structure: one short block per line. For funding stability: income
by source for two years.

**"Leadership" questions (fellowships and awards).** Echoing Green asks for concrete examples of setting a vision,
driving it to a goal, and building coalitions; its older page adds resilience and the ability to attract resources.
Diana Award judges commitment, impact, ownership of the work, and obstacles overcome. Structure: situation → what
you did → result → what you learned.

### F. Partners

**"Who will be involved and what are their roles?"** (Comic Relief: 2,100 characters; Enabel scores partner
involvement.) Tests: real roles. Structure: partner → role → share of budget if any → why them. Comic Relief only
wants partners that hold part of the budget listed as formal partners, and asks for clear roles and power
relationships.

### G. Money, risk and the future

**"Budget and budget justification"** → section 3.

**"Risks and mitigation"** (MacArthur scores a mitigation plan for staff, political, market and social change.)
Structure: risk → likelihood → effect → mitigation → early warning sign.

**"Sustainability / what happens after the grant?"** Structure: financial (who pays next) → institutional (who owns
it locally) → policy (what lasting change in rules or practice) → environmental, if relevant. One line each minimum.

### H. Media and extras

**Video.** Comic Relief accepts an optional 2-minute link (phone-quality is fine; not password-protected; about
this request, not a general film); longer films are not watched. Echoing Green requires a 60-second "why do you do
what you do" video. Structure: name and partners → what the money does → what it changes.

**Referees.** Pollination contacts an international applicant's referee only at the final stage; no letter needed.
Diana Award needs a professional nominator, two professional supporters (no family or friends) and three pieces of
evidence. Self-nomination is not allowed.

## 3. Budgets and documents

### Budget conventions

- **Mirror the narrative, line by line.** Enabel doubles "activities adequately reflected in the budget" and
  separately scores the cost-to-results ratio. A budget is read as capacity evidence.
- **Indirect costs are now often expected, not hidden.** Ford's minimum indirect rate on project grants is 25% since
  January 2023 (up from 20%), higher with documentation. MacArthur adds 29% on top of project costs for non-profit
  project grants (not on general support or its expedited small grants); applicants do not itemise it. MacArthur
  counts admin and fundraising staff, admin rent, utilities for those functions, insurance and bank fees as
  indirect; shared costs (a share of rent, utilities) allocated to the project are direct and must be itemised.
  Other funders cap or forbid overhead: Commonwealth funds no core costs (capacity work up to 5% of the grant after
  shortlisting). Read the call; then cost honestly. Plinth advises full cost recovery and showing the calculation
  for every line.
- **Caps relative to income.** Commonwealth: ask no more than 70% of average annual income over two years, and
  income under £2.5 million. (Third-party summaries of Comic Relief give a 40% cap and an income band; not
  verified on a Comic Relief page.) A small organisation can be ineligible because it is small.
- **Currency.** Budget in the funder's currency unless told otherwise. Japan's GGP caps at 10 million yen and warns
  applicants to leave margin for yen–dollar–naira movement. The Global Fund's budgeting rules (via UNDP) ask for a
  best-estimate rate, the method disclosed in the assumptions, official rates where fixed, and no exchange-rate
  contingency line. GCIP Nigeria pays in naira at the CBN rate. State the rate, its source and its date.
- **In-kind.** Value it with a written calculation and keep proof (letter of support, handover note). Pollination
  rewards creative use of non-cash resources. The Bolton factsheet notes that funders ask what happens to equipment:
  storage, insurance, use.
- **Co-funding.** develoPPP caps public money at 50%; GSMA has needed 50% match (unconfirmed for 2026 calls). OSIWA has been reported (search
  summaries of old calls, not verified) to part-fund and ask the applicant to raise the balance. Enabel checks that the ask has not moved more than 20% from
  concept note to full proposal.
- **No retrospective costs.** Very few funders pay for work already done; decisions take months (Lottery: about
  16 weeks; Pollination: 6–12 weeks; Commonwealth: about nine months from close to outcome).
- **Quotes.** Cost lines from real quotes; round-number estimates and below-market salaries read as weak planning
  (Plinth). Inflated budgets are a known decline reason (Lottery).
- **What small funds will not pay.** Pollination: no pay to the grantee or family, no political work, no
  pass-through donations. Commonwealth: no training for government officials, no service delivery, no partisan
  communication. GGP: equipment and works, completed within a year.

## 4. Platforms and form mechanics

| Platform | Seen at | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| SurveyMonkey Apply (SM Apply) | Commonwealth Foundation; LEAP NYFF | Work is split into tasks: forms, file uploads, recommendations, external tasks. All required tasks must be complete. Recommendation requests go to referees through the system and can trigger submission. Collaborators can be added without sharing a login. Admins can limit applications per person |
| Submittable | Google.org Impact Challenge 2026 | Search summaries of several funders' FAQs (pages not opened): autosave plus a manual save; counters include spaces and turn red over the limit, blocking submit but not save; collaborators can edit but only the owner submits, and simultaneous editing can overwrite; edits after submission only if the funder reopens the form |
| Fluxx | Azrieli and many US foundations | Register first; approval can take time. No autosave on Azrieli's portal: press Save. A saved draft is never reviewed; only a submitted one. Character limits per field (Azrieli: 600 for summary and relevance). Withdrawal and resubmission possible |
| EU Funding & Tenders Portal | EU Delegation calls | Organisation needs a PIC. Two stages: concept note, then full application. Annexes and corrigenda change during the call (the 2026 Nigeria call had guidelines updated in May): reread before submitting |
| UN Partner Portal | UNICEF and nine other UN bodies | Register the organisation once; respond to calls for expressions of interest from it |
| Google Forms | Many Nigerian and youth calls | Respondents sign in to a Google account; editing after submission works only if the owner enabled it. Draft from a document, paste in last |
| Email submission | Japan GGP Nigeria | Subject line in the embassy's stated format; all documents attached |
| Bespoke forms | Comic Relief, Lottery, Pollination | Comic Relief's form autosaves, can lose work if open in two tabs, flags unanswered required questions, and branches (answers unlock further questions; complete in order). Pollination takes no emailed applications |

Mechanics that apply everywhere:

1. **Draft offline, paste last.** Comic Relief publishes its questions and limits so applicants can write offline.
2. **Count characters with spaces.** Limits are characters on Comic Relief and Azrieli, words on others. Text over
   the limit is ignored by DWP and blocked by Submittable.
3. **Answer the guidance bullets.** Comic Relief's guidance under each question is the hidden grid; the Lottery says
   to answer each bullet in a sentence or two.
4. **Choices that cannot be undone.** The AU-EU Youth Action Lab warns that grant type and language chosen at
   registration cannot be changed. Echoing Green allows no edits after submission.
5. **One application per programme.** Comic Relief reads only one proposal per organisation per programme;
   Commonwealth lets an organisation appear in only one application; Echoing Green allows one per organisation per
   cycle. You may be a named partner elsewhere (Comic Relief).
6. **Submit early.** Comic Relief accepts no late proposals; Commonwealth's page gave two different closing times
   (12pm and 1pm GMT): use the earlier one.
7. **Keep a copy and the confirmation ID** of every submission.

## 5. Failure reasons and fixes

| # | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Not eligible: legal form, geography, age of organisation, income band (Bolton CVS; Commonwealth's three-year and income rules) | Build the gate checklist from the call before writing a word |
| 2 | Off-priority: the funder's aim is not met (Bolton; GrantWatch-type summaries; Alliance insider) | Name one priority in the funder's words; drop the call if fit needs stretching |
| 3 | Guidance ignored (the Lottery's main complaint, via Connected Voice) | One sentence per guidance bullet |
| 4 | Activities written as impact (Lottery) | Each outcome is a change in people, with a measure |
| 5 | No monitoring plan, no evidence of past work (Lottery; Bolton) | Indicators, baseline, source, who collects; one past result with its record |
| 6 | Need asserted, not shown; community not involved (Big Lottery via Bolton; Lottery) | Local data plus a consultation count and what people said |
| 7 | Budget unrealistic, inflated or unexplained (Plinth; Lottery; Bolton) | Quotes and unit costs; full cost recovery shown |
| 8 | Weak credibility: generic "about us" (Plinth) | Dated achievements with numbers and named partners |
| 9 | Story without data, or data without people (Plinth; Alliance) | Claim, then evidence, then one illustration |
| 10 | Incomplete or missing documents; bank statement the most common gap, by one directory's account (Bolton; Lottery summary) | Document pack ready before drafting |
| 11 | Sustainability skipped or vague (MacArthur) | Financial, institutional, policy lines answered |
| 12 | Retrospective request; deadline missed (Bolton) | Work back from the decision date |
| 13 | Project changed without telling the funder; a bid writer as the main contact (Connected Voice) | The founder is the contact; changes agreed before they happen |
| 14 | Capacity line below the floor (Enabel) | Prior grants, staff, systems and income sources stated plainly |
| 15 | Same text sent everywhere (Plinth; Chartered Institute of Fundraising advice cited there: 8–12 researched bids, not 30 generic ones) | Tailor; the desk's `originality.py` rule already enforces this |

What program officers and funders say wins:
- Talk to the funder before applying; those who do avoid common pitfalls (Lottery Northern Ireland, cited in search
  results). Treat officers as allies, answer their questions, weigh their advice (Alliance).
- Write so a board member outside the field follows it.
- Ask for feedback after a decline and address it before reapplying; reapply with new data after 6–12 months, not
  a near copy (Plinth; Lottery). Pollination lets declined applicants reapply if they disclose the earlier attempt.
- Echoing Green gives non-selected applicants their numeric scores, not comments: use them.
