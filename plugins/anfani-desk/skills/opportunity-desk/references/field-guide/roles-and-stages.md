# Roles and stages: a field guide for jobs and speaking applications

Researched October 2026. Deadlines, rules and policies change: check each programme's own page before acting.

How to fill any job application or call for proposals well. Built from 92 web sources read on 15 October 2026
(listed in section 6). This file holds the mechanics, the question bank and the structures; the applicant's own
answers and facts come only from the profile file `desk/profile.md`.

Two words used throughout. **Screen**: the first pass, often a machine or a rule. **Panel**: the humans who score
what survives the screen.

---

## 1. How hiring managers and committees decide

**Most decisions are made against a sheet the applicant never sees.** Write to that sheet.

- **Jobs are scored on fixed criteria.** Greenhouse scorecards list attributes chosen when the job is opened. Each
  interviewer rates them and gives one of four verdicts, from Definitely Not to Strong Yes (Greenhouse support).
  Google's structured-interview guide pairs a fixed set of questions with a rubric. The rubric runs from poor through
  borderline and solid to outstanding (re:Work).
- **Panels score alone first.** Greenhouse found that interviewers who saw a colleague's score first were 3.6% more
  likely to copy it. New accounts now hide other scores until you submit your own. Pretalx gives reviewers the same
  setting. So each reader must find the evidence on their own. Repeat the key proof in every document.
- **Machines filter on rules, not on judgement.** Workday, Taleo, LinkedIn and Inspira can reject a candidate
  automatically when a screening answer misses a set threshold (Leonstaff, LinkedIn Help, Tufts). Ashby's AI marks
  each criterion as meets, does not meet or undecided. It cites the line it relied on, and a human decides (Ashby
  docs). Lever does not score at all. Recruiters search it, and it does not expand acronyms (Jobscan).
- **The first look is short.** The Ladders eye-tracking study measured about 7.4 seconds on a first pass. Simple
  layouts with clear headings did best (HR Dive). ERE's critique is fair: that number is the time to decide whether
  to keep reading, not the whole review. Plan for both readers. The top third must win the skim. The rest must
  survive a careful read.
- **The UN system scores competencies.** A UN panel asks for past behaviour, scored against the competencies named in
  the vacancy. The current UN framework has four values and five behaviours, listed in section 2.4 (CEPAL). Generic
  passion earns nothing. Mandate fit and defensible results do (MDB Jobs).
- **Conference committees score four things.** Content, originality, relevance and the speaker, usually on a 1 to 5
  scale (KubeCon, Write the Docs). KubeCon tells reviewers to say yes to only about 30%. Many committees review blind
  at first: PyCon AU strips names, and SREcon and pretalx can hide them. The title and abstract must carry the
  proposal without the bio.
- **Committees test from the seat.** Opensource.com's reviewer test asks what an audience member would gain. It
  rates on title and abstract alone, because that is all an attendee sees.
- **Vendor pitches lose.** KubeCon says reviewers rate promotional talks very poorly. TEDx bans selling your own
  product, book or employer. Africa Tech Summit puts vendors in sponsored slots, apart from editorial ones.
- **TEDx picks an idea, not a person.** TED's organiser guide asks for an idea that is new or surprising and that
  draws a larger conclusion from evidence. A story alone is not enough. Local voices come first. Circuit speakers
  and commercial speakers are avoided.

## 2. Jobs

### 2.1 Question bank: what each tests, and the answer structure

The applicant's own answers live in `desk/profile.md`. Use these structures to fit them to a job form.

**A. Motivation**

| Question | What it tests | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Why this role? | That you read the job, not just the title | Name two duties from the posting. Give your proof for each in one sentence. Then the one thing you want to build in the role |
| Why this organisation? | Mandate fit (UN, foundations), product fit (tech) | Use their words once. Name one programme, product or strategy line. Say what you would add to it. No praise (MDB Jobs) |
| Why now / why leave your current role? | Stability and honest direction | One fact about the change. One sentence on direction. Never criticise a past employer |
| Tell us about yourself (form or interview opener) | Positioning, not a CV recital | Impactpool's three parts: who you are professionally, the value you bring, why this role now. 60 to 90 seconds. Choose three things you want remembered and build back from them |

**B. Competency (behavioural) questions.** Map each one to the vacancy's named competencies. Use STAR. Spend the
least time on Situation and Task and the most on Action, using "I" (Indeed). Close on a Result with a number you can
defend, plus one line on what changed after. Aim for 1.5 to 2 minutes spoken, or 150 to 250 words written. Inspira
duty entries allow 1,000 characters and use C-A-R (context, action, result) (Tufts).

| Competency (old UN name / current UN behaviour) | Typical prompt | What the panel listens for |
|---|---|---|
| Teamwork / Connect and collaborate | A time you worked with people who disagreed | Who disagreed, what you did to bring them in, what the team delivered, whose credit it was |
| Communication | A time you explained something complex to a non-specialist | The audience, what you cut, how you checked they understood |
| Planning and organising / Analyse and plan | A time you managed competing deadlines | How you ranked the work, what you dropped and why, the evidence you used |
| Accountability / Deliver results with positive impact | A time something went wrong on your watch | Ownership in the first sentence, the fix, the process change, no blame |
| Creativity / Adapt and innovate | A time you changed how something was done | The old way, the cost, the new way, the adoption |
| Client orientation | A time a user or partner was unhappy | What they needed, not what they said; the follow-up |
| Continuous learning / Learn and develop | A skill you taught yourself | Why, how, how fast, and where you used it |
| Leadership (managerial posts) | A time you led without authority | The decision, who followed, what went wrong, the result. Impactpool's senior bar: deciding with limited time, resources or information |
| Integrity / ethics | A time you were pressed to cut a corner | The pressure, the line you held, the cost you accepted |

Build a story bank of five to eight stories. Tag each with the competencies it proves. The UN interview guides
suggest two or three examples per competency in the posting. One story can serve several prompts if the Action
changes.

**C. Hypothetical (situational) questions.** "What would you do if..." These test reasoning, not the right answer
(re:Work). Structure: say what you would find out first, list the options, choose one, name the risk, say how you
would know it worked. If you have done it before, say so and turn it into a behavioural answer.

**D. Technical questions** (engineering, digital, data roles)

| Question | What it tests | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Describe a system you built | Depth, trade-offs, ownership | The problem, the constraint, the architecture in three parts, one choice you would change, the result |
| Link to code or portfolio | Real work that matches the claim | Three links at most. One line on each: what it is and what you did. Lever keeps links live in the CV (Jobscan) |
| Take-home task | Scoping, communication, working code | See 2.6 |
| Skill self-ratings and years per tool | Honest calibration; often a knockout | Count only real use. Under-claiming beats a claim that fails in the live test |

**E. Logistics fields.** Often scored by rules. Answer exactly.

| Field | What happens to it | How to answer |
|---|---|---|
| Work authorisation / sponsorship | A frequent knockout in Workday, LinkedIn and Taleo | Answer for the country named in the question. If the role is remote from Nigeria, say so in the free-text box: authorised to work in Nigeria, no sponsorship needed to work from there. Gates Foundation sponsors visas only for some leadership roles |
| Desired salary | Can be a knockout when above the budget (Leonstaff) | Leave it blank if optional. If required, give a researched range for the role's location and currency, and say "negotiable on total package" where a notes box exists. Never give salary history unless forced |
| Years of experience | Totalled by the system (Inspira) and compared to the minimum | Count full-time, part-time, contract, volunteer and internship work where the form allows it (Tufts, Leonstaff) |
| Languages | Inspira screens on each of four skills against the posting | Match each skill to the level you can prove |
| Notice period / start date | Planning; sometimes a knockout | A date, not "ASAP". Check the current contract first |
| Relocation / onsite | Knockout for hybrid roles | Honest yes or no; add conditions in notes |
| Misconduct, criminal and conflict-of-interest questions | Legal record | Answer truthfully. UN forms ask about discipline and harassment findings (Tufts) |
| Diversity and EEO | Not scored in most systems | Optional; the applicant's choice each time |

**F. Values and mission prompts** (foundations, Ashoka, Mastercard Foundation)

- Ashoka hires all year against its criteria and runs a chain of conversations it calls One Search (search summary;
  page blocked). Prepare an account of one idea you turned into reality, with the people and evidence behind it.
- Mastercard Foundation internships ask for proof of community engagement and leadership beyond study. Candidates
  must be eligible to work in one of its African offices, Nigeria among them (NDLink).
- World Bank YPP: a longlist of the top 10%, then panel, case and group exercises. The panel scores technical depth,
  judgement, self-reflection and motivation. There is also a written reflection (World Bank).

### 2.2 ATS mechanics that change how you write

1. **Fill the form fields as if the CV did not exist.** Taleo and Workday search and filter on the structured
   fields. Recruiters may never open the attached file (ATSVerification Taleo, Hireflow). Re-type titles, employers
   and dates exactly.
2. **Check the parsed profile before submitting.** Parsers put dates and titles in the wrong boxes, mostly from
   sidebars and columns. A new upload may not overwrite old fields (Hireflow).
3. **One column, standard headings, selectable text.** Keep contact details in the body, not the page header.
   Leave out icons, skill bars and tables (ATSVerification SmartRecruiters, Jobscan). Use PDF unless the posting or
   an older Taleo asks for .docx.
4. **Write acronyms both ways.** "Search engine optimisation (SEO)", "content management system (CMS)". Lever search
   does not expand abbreviations (Jobscan).
5. **Mirror the posting's nouns where they are true.** Recruiters search by title, skill and tool. Ashby's AI reads
   for evidence of each criterion. Matching words alone does not pass it.
6. **You can rarely edit after submitting.** LinkedIn Easy Apply, Workday and Taleo lock the application (Jobscan,
   Leonstaff). Save drafts outside the browser. Inspira times out, so save often (Tufts).
7. **Your history follows you.** Workday and Lever link past applications to the same employer. Keep dates
   consistent across every application.
8. **Respect the AI disclosure.** Ashby lets candidates opt out of automated review. Some employers say they want the
   letter written by the candidate (Indeed). Record the employer's AI policy on the answer sheet, as the desk already
   does for grants.

### 2.3 Cover letters that work

**Evidence that they are read.** Resume Genius surveyed 625 US hiring managers. 83% said they read most letters.
45% read the letter before the CV. 72% expect one even when it is optional. Their preferred length was about 400
words. Treat these figures as vendor survey data.

**Structure (about 300 to 400 words; one page; 400 to 600 for a UN letter):**

1. **Opening (2 to 3 sentences).** The role, and the one result that makes you relevant, in the first line. Never
   "I am writing to apply". Avoid opening with "I am".
2. **Match paragraph (the core).** Take the two or three hardest requirements from the posting. For each, give one
   proof: what you did, for whom, and the result. For a UN or INGO letter, use the competency names from the vacancy
   (MDB Jobs).
3. **Why them (2 to 3 sentences).** One specific thing about the organisation's current work and what you would add.
   Mandate fit, not praise.
4. **Close.** Availability, location or work-authorisation status if it helps, and a plain request for the
   conversation.

Rules: one letter per role. Name the hiring manager if you can find one (Indeed). No new facts that the CV
contradicts. When Easy Apply has no letter field, merge the letter into the CV PDF as page one (Jobscan). Inspira's
motivation statement allows 2,000 characters, or 1,000 at some agencies. Draft it in a document first (Tufts).

### 2.4 CV and résumé rules

- **Lengths.** Tech and startup roles: one to two pages. INGO and UN: two to three pages, or the platform profile
  (Inspira PHP, P.11). Europass is the common format in European and EU-funded hiring and offers 31 languages
  (Europass).
- **Top third.** Name, location and time zone, email, phone, LinkedIn, GitHub or portfolio. Then a three-line summary
  that names the three sides of the work (code, WordPress, AI automation) and one result.
- **Bullets.** Action verb, what you did, scope, result. Quantify only what you can defend in an interview (MDB
  Jobs). Translate duties into competency language for the UN system.
- **Dates.** Month and year. No overlaps the reader cannot explain. Match the platform profile exactly (MDB Jobs).
  Explain gaps (Europass).
- **Photo.** Expected in parts of Europe and Africa. Omit it for US, UK and most tech roles unless asked.
- **Skills.** Plain text, grouped by side. No bars or star ratings.
- **References line.** "Available on request" wastes a line. Use the space for proof.
- **The UN's current values and behaviours** (CEPAL, from the UN Values and Behaviours Framework):
  values: inclusion, integrity, humility, humanity. Behaviours: connect and collaborate; analyse and plan; deliver
  results with positive impact; learn and develop; adapt and innovate. Older vacancies still use the 2010 list:
  communication, teamwork, planning and organising, accountability, creativity, client orientation, continuous
  learning, technological awareness. Use whichever list the vacancy names.

### 2.5 References

- UN agencies check references after the interview. They speak to former supervisors about behaviour, not only
  dates (search summary of the NOREC guide).
- Brief every referee: the role, the two or three competencies the employer is testing, the stories you want
  confirmed, and the likely call window. Keep the referee list and their consent in `desk/profile.md`.
- Choose referees who supervised the work the role needs. For engineering, someone who saw the code. For programme
  roles, a partner who saw the delivery.

### 2.6 Take-home tasks

- **Fair size** is a few hours. More than a day should be questioned (EdSurge). Ask the hiring manager how long they
  expect it to take, then set your own time limit (EdSurge, This Dot).
- **Reviewers read the README first.** Include what you built, how to run it, the decisions and trade-offs, the tests,
  and what you would do with more time (This Dot).
- **Working and simple beats ambitious and half-done.** Over-engineering is a common failure. Be ready to defend every
  tool you chose (This Dot).
- **It continues in the interview.** Expect to critique your own work, say what you would cut and name the shortcuts
  you took (Holloway).
- **Speed is part of the signal.** A Dropbox manager quoted by Holloway saw about 20% drop out and roughly 10% pass.
  Finish, on time.

### 2.7 Interviews

- **Recruiter screen**, then **hiring manager**, then **team panel** of 45 to 60 minutes each. This is the Gates
  Foundation sequence, and most foundations follow it.
- **UN shape.** Screen against minimum requirements, then a written or technical test, then a competency panel of at
  least three, then references (search summaries of the UNDP pages). The written test follows the job description.
- **Recorded video interviews** (UNDP, some INGOs, AI-scored platforms). Keep answers short: context, action,
  result. Practise on camera. Check light, sound and connection (Impactpool).
- **Fellowship-style assessment centres** (World Bank YPP): case study, group exercise, written reflection.
- **Remote talent networks** test in stages. Toptal: English and personality, then skills, then a live screen, then
  a one-to-three-week test project. About 3% pass. Andela: English test, technical assessment, live interview, then
  client interviews. Turing: profile, skill tests, live interview.

### 2.8 Remote roles from Africa

- Most job ads mean "remote within a country or region". Check the location tag. Remotive separates "available from
  my location" from "explicitly in my location". Remote OK filters by African country.
- Employers hire from Nigeria in three ways: as a contractor, through an employer of record, or through a local
  entity. Contractor or employee status depends on how the work is actually done, not the contract's title (Zoho).
  Ask which one the employer uses before answering the sponsorship questions.
- Nigerian employment basics to know before signing: notice is tied to length of service. Probation is not defined in
  law. Inventions by an employee or contractor are treated as the employer's. Non-competes are usually capped at two
  years (Native Teams). Get IP terms in writing, especially for any side business or freelance work.
- Power and internet are real costs. Employers that hire in Nigeria budget for allowances (Zoho). Raising it is
  normal, not a weakness.

## 3. Speaking

### 3.1 CFP question bank

| Field | What reviewers use it for | How to fill it |
|---|---|---|
| Title | First filter; often scored alone | Specific, plain, one promise. A question or a turn of phrase is fine if the topic stays clear (SQLBits). Title counts for more than the abstract (swyx) |
| Short abstract (public) | What attendees and blind reviewers see | The formula in 3.2. 3 to 6 sentences (Snyk) |
| Long description / outline | Can the talk fill the slot | Sections with minutes. Recommended even when optional (ADSecurity, GitNation) |
| Learning outcomes | Will attendees leave able to do something | "After this session, attendees will be able to..." with verbs, not topics (OpenWater, citing ASAE) |
| Audience and level | Fit with the track | Name the person: role, experience, the problem they bring (Ozar, USENIX) |
| Format | Room planning | Talk, panel, workshop, lab, fireside chat. Panels: two to three speakers plus a moderator, varied by gender, company and region. KubeCon requires at least one panellist who is not a man |
| Notes to reviewers | Context they cannot publish | Why now, why you, what is new if given before, any link to a recording |
| Has this been presented before? | Originality score | Say where, and what has changed (ADSecurity). Write the Docs and KubeCon mark down repeat talks |
| Speaker bio (often hidden in round one) | Speaker fit, after the blind round | See 3.4 |
| Video of past speaking | Delivery risk | A short clip is enough (ADSecurity) |
| Sponsor or vendor status | Separates paid slots from editorial ones | Declare it. Africa Tech Summit asks vendors to say so |
| Travel and fee support needed | Budget | Ask plainly. A need for support rarely affects the content score |
| Accessibility, recording consent, co-speakers | Logistics | Fill in every field. Incomplete forms are discarded (ADSecurity, Agile Testing Days) |

### 3.2 Abstract formula

Five moves, 80 to 150 words.

1. **Pain or tension (1 sentence).** A problem the audience already feels. Opening on the problem beats opening on
   takeaways (Snyk, Security Boulevard).
2. **Why now (1 sentence).** What changed, or what everyone gets wrong.
3. **Your evidence (1 to 2 sentences).** What you built or ran, and one concrete detail. Story-led proposals feel
   relatable (GitNation). Keep it about the work, not the résumé.
4. **What they leave with (2 to 3 items).** Things they can do. Not "this session will cover".
5. **Who it is for and the format (1 sentence).**

Don'ts: opening with "In this talk I will"; teasers that hide the content (USENIX); buzzword stacks (Agile Testing
Days); promising more than the slot can hold (SQLBits); naming your product in the title.

**Title patterns that work:** the claim ("Africa's talent problem is a distribution problem"); the how-to with a
constraint ("Shipping a payments app with two engineers and no QA team"); the confession ("What 200 bootcamp
graduates taught me about free training"). Test it: would someone choose this room from the title alone?

### 3.3 Reviewer criteria you can design for

| Criterion | Where it comes from | Design move |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance to this audience | KubeCon, Write the Docs, PyCon DE (search summary) | Use the event's track names and theme words. Name the attendee |
| Originality | KubeCon, Write the Docs, SXSW EDU | Your own data and field experience; a view not given elsewhere |
| Soundness, focus | KubeCon | One idea. TEDx organisers distrust applications that make five points (search summary) |
| Takeaways | PyCon DE (search summary), OpenWater | Concrete, measurable "able to" outcomes |
| Speaker fit | All | Bio lines that match the topic only |
| Program balance | Write the Docs, KubeCon | You cannot control it. Submit two to three distinct proposals to raise the odds (swyx, Security Boulevard), but not a flood (Agile Testing Days). Many events accept only one talk per speaker |
| Not a sales pitch | KubeCon, TEDx rules, Africa Tech Summit | Products appear only as evidence for the idea |

Scoring scales differ. Write the Docs uses 1 to 5 with no 3s and ranks by median. PyCon AU uses −2 to +2, blind in
round one, with the speaker revealed in round two. Pretalx allows weighted score categories and can hide reviews.
Speakers see reviews only if the organiser puts them in the decision email. Plan for silence, not feedback.

### 3.4 Bios

- **Third person for anything the event prints** (Sessionboard, Oxford Abstracts). First person stays on the speaker's own
  channels.
- **Keep four lengths ready:** about 50 words (programme grid), 100 (website and app), 200 (keynote page), and a
  30-second spoken intro for the moderator (Sessionboard). Keep the approved versions in `desk/profile.md`.
  Copy them; do not rewrite.
- **Open with why this person fits this topic.** Cut any line that only shows seniority. Never dump a timeline
  (Sessionboard).
- **The moderator intro** ends with the name, as the cue to clap. Include phonetic pronunciation of the speaker's name,
  confirmed by the speaker.
- Keep a headshot at 1200 px or larger and the current title on file (Sessionboard).

### 3.5 Speaker one-sheet and panel pitch

**One-sheet (one page or a link):** photo and name; a 100-word bio; three talk topics written as audience
outcomes; who each topic is for; past stages with dates; a video link; one or two testimonials; contact and booking
details (search summary of speaker-bureau guides). A hosted page works better than a PDF attachment.

**Panel pitch (to an organiser):** the question the panel settles, in one line; why the disagreement is real; three
named panellists with one line each on the angle they bring (varied by sector, gender and country); the moderator;
the format and minutes; what the audience leaves with. SXSW EDU asks how each speaker adds diversity. KubeCon marks
down panels from a single company.

### 3.6 TEDx applications

- **The idea, not the CV.** The idea should be new, or an old idea argued in a fresh way, and it should draw a
  conclusion from evidence (TED organiser guide). A personal story must lead to a bigger point (TEDxHartford, search
  summary).
- **Rules:** speakers are not paid and are never charged. Talks run under 18 minutes, ideally 3 to 9. Nothing
  commercial, political or religious. Science claims need vetted evidence. No panels, no Q&A (TEDx rules).
- **Local first.** Organisers prefer local voices and avoid circuit speakers (TED guide). TEDx events in the speaker's
  own city and region are the natural first targets.
- **Applications attract commercial speakers, so organisers also scout.** A short, specific email to the organiser
  can work better than a form (search summary of TED guidance).
- **Timing:** many events close speaker calls four to six months ahead (search summary). If they ask for a one-minute
  video, send one minute.

### 3.7 Fees, travel and red flags

- **Most CFP talks are unpaid.** Many include a free pass; some cover travel. Keynotes and invited talks can carry a
  fee. Bureau ranges: education and youth $1,000 to $10,000; keynotes from $5,000 (Gotham Artists). Treat these as
  US-market figures.
- Ask in writing what is covered: fee, flights, hotel nights, visa letter, registration.
- **Pay-to-speak is a red flag.** A late invitation, a title you do not hold, an invitation to a second event in an
  unrelated field, and speakers paying their own way plus a fee are all warning signs. At such events most of the
  people in the room turn out to be speakers (BMJ). Sponsored slots at real events (Africa Tech Summit, GITEX) are
  a different thing: they are paid marketing. Label them that way.

### 3.8 After you submit

- Expect four to six weeks of review. PyCon AU ran two rounds over about a month. Acceptances need confirming within
  days. Rejections often go out only after the programme is final. Africa Tech Summit contacts only successful
  applicants.
- If rejected, ask politely for feedback. If two people say the same thing, rewrite (Security Boulevard).
- Keep a talk bank: titles, abstracts in three lengths, bios, a headshot and video links. Re-aim proposals rather
  than start from blank (swyx).
- Deliver the talk you proposed. Switching topics on stage ends future invitations (ADSecurity).
- Within a week: thank the organiser, share the recording or slides, and log the outcome in
  the application tracker.

## 4. Platform quirks

| Platform | Used by | Quirks that change what we do |
|---|---|---|
| Workday | Large employers, many foundations and INGOs | Knockout questions reject automatically. Parsed fields are often wrong, so check every row. Earlier applications to the same employer are linked. You cannot edit after submitting (Leonstaff, Hireflow) |
| Greenhouse | Tech companies, some nonprofits | Scorecards with focus attributes and a four-step verdict. Interviewers usually cannot see others' scores until they submit (Greenhouse) |
| Lever | Startups | Accepts PDF, Word, RTF, HTML and ODF; cannot read images. No acronym expansion. Recruiters score by hand. Links in the CV stay live (Jobscan) |
| Ashby | Newer tech companies | AI marks each criterion meets, does not meet or undecided, with a citation. No ranking. Candidates can opt out. Long-form answers are checked for completion (Ashby docs, Ashby AI) |
| SmartRecruiters | Mid-size and large employers | Standard and job-specific screening questions. Two-column layouts and image text break parsing. Keep contact details in the body (ATSVerification) |
| Taleo (Oracle) | Older enterprise, public bodies | Re-enter work history in form fields; those fields are what recruiters search. Older setups may prefer .docx. Knockout questionnaires (ATSVerification Taleo) |
| BambooHR | Small and mid-size companies | Custom required questions; automatic confirmation email. Parsing could not be confirmed (search summary). Answer every custom question in full |
| Jobvite | Mid-market and enterprise | Parsing and candidate-facing status pages (search summary; vendor pages blocked) |
| LinkedIn Easy Apply | Everyone | Up to 13 screening question types, including work authorisation, visa status and skill expertise. Must-have answers can auto-archive with a rejection email. The profile is read before the CV, and the CV often gets less weight. No letter field, so merge the letter into the PDF. No edits after submitting (LinkedIn Help, Jobscan) |
| UN Inspira / careers.un.org | UN Secretariat | Screening questions appear only on a live job. Years of experience are totalled by the system. Each of the four language skills is screened. Duty entries allow 1,000 characters. Motivation statement allows 2,000 (or 1,000). Times out, so save often. Download the PHP as a PDF (Tufts) |
| Impactpool | UN agencies, INGOs, EU bodies | Aggregator with matching; the paid tier holds guides and sample questions. Applications usually land on the agency's own portal |
| Devex | Development sector | Jobs board with paid tiers (site unreachable on 15 Oct; check before relying on it) |
| Remote talent networks (Andela, Toptal, Turing) | Engineers | Staged tests before any client sees you. Toptal's test project runs one to three weeks |
| Sessionize | Most tech conferences | One speaker profile reused across events. Organisers choose an evaluation mode, including star rating (Sessionize). Keep the profile bio neutral and tailor each session |
| Pretalx | Open-source, Python and docs events | Weighted score categories; blind review per phase; organisers can redact proposal text; reviews hidden from speakers by default (pretalx docs, Write the Docs) |
| PaperCall | Some developer conferences | Site unreachable on 15 Oct. Treat as a standard CFP form and keep the answers in the talk bank |
| Google Forms and custom forms (AOT Lagos, AfricArena, Lagos Startup Week) | African events | Often only identity fields: AOT Lagos asks name, company, title, LinkedIn and location. The LinkedIn profile is the proposal, so keep it current. Most have no save function, so draft offline (desk practice) |
| SXSW PanelPicker | SXSW, SXSW EDU | Public submission and a community vote; staff and advisers make the final choice. SXSW 2027 runs 15 to 21 March in Austin, and voting has opened (sxsw.com). SXSW EDU asks how each panellist adds diversity |
| TEDx forms | Local TEDx events | Idea-first questions, often a one-minute video. Organiser outreach matters (TED) |
